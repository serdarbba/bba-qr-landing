Swarm UAV — Industrial Multi-Drone Operations

A single drone is a tool. A coordinated swarm is an infrastructure. For industrial clients — energy companies, logistics operators, port authorities, military — the real value comes from deploying multiple drones simultaneously across large areas, with no manual coordination required.

10×
Coverage area compared to a single-drone deployment
0
Human operators needed per drone once mission is active
24/7
Continuous operation via autonomous dock-and-recharge cycles

What the swarm system does for an industrial customer

  • One operator launches a mission — the software assigns areas, roles, and routes to each drone automatically
  • Drones maintain coordinated formations (line, V, grid, circle) and adapt if one unit fails or returns to dock
  • Each drone in the swarm runs its own AI vision — detections from all drones are merged into a single live picture
  • If a drone's battery runs low, the swarm re-routes to maintain coverage without any human input
  • All swarm data flows into the GCS dashboard — one screen, entire operation
Perimeter Security at Scale

A 40km industrial perimeter that would require 20+ guards can be continuously monitored by a swarm of 4–6 drones rotating through dock recharge. Any intrusion triggers immediate AI alert and drone re-routing to the zone.

→ Replaces static guard posts. Operates at night, in rain, without fatigue.
Port & Logistics Yard Oversight

Container yards, warehouses, and cargo terminals require constant situational awareness. A coordinated swarm maps vehicle movements, monitors loading zones, and flags anomalies — all without interrupting ground operations.

→ Live aerial picture of your entire yard, updated every minute.
Energy Infrastructure Patrol

Solar farms, wind turbine fields, pipelines, and power lines span hundreds of kilometers. Swarm patrol divides the route and runs continuously, flagging thermal anomalies, damage, and unauthorized access in real-time.

→ Fault detection before it becomes failure. Predictive, not reactive.
Search & Emergency Response

When response time matters, swarm deployment covers maximum ground in minimum time. Each drone feeds live video and AI detections back to the command center, with GPS positions of persons or hazards marked automatically.

→ Minutes to deploy, seconds to detect. No manual review of footage.

The AI-Controlled Operation — Where This Is Heading

Today's drone operations still rely on human pilots for most decisions. BBA's roadmap eliminates that dependency layer by layer. The end state is a drone that receives a mission objective in plain language and executes it completely — plan, fly, observe, decide, report — without human intervention at any step.

Live Today

Natural Language Mission Control

An operator types "patrol the north perimeter and alert me if you see any vehicles after 22:00." The AI GCS translates this into flight routes, triggers detection rules, and sends alerts. No joystick, no manual waypoints.

Live Today

Real-Time Risk Intelligence

The system monitors every drone continuously — battery health, navigation stability, sensor quality, wind load — and calculates a compound risk score every 500ms. It acts before the operator notices a problem.

Active Development

Fully Autonomous Indoor Operations

Drones navigating GPS-denied environments — warehouses, tunnels, buildings — using only onboard cameras and AI. No external infrastructure, no markers, no pre-mapping required. The drone builds its own map as it flies.

Active Development

Self-Sustaining Patrol Loops

Drone launches, patrols, docks autonomously to recharge, relaunches — indefinitely. Human involvement is limited to reviewing AI-generated incident reports. Operations continue overnight, on weekends, without staff.

Roadmap

Cross-Swarm Decision Making

If Drone A detects an event, it autonomously re-tasks Drone B for closer inspection while Drone C maintains coverage of the original area. The swarm redistributes itself without human direction.

Roadmap

Predictive Mission Scheduling

The AI learns from historical flight data — when incidents typically occur, which zones are high-risk, what weather patterns reduce effectiveness — and proactively schedules patrols to maximize impact.
